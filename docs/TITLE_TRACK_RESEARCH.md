# Title track research (2026-10-20)

Why v1-v5 missed: v1/v5 had no melody to remember; v2/v4 had melody but no weight and blurred Sanskrit; v3 had weight
but no diction. None had a *hook*: a short musical idea that returns and that a listener would hum.

## What makes a title theme work
- **A hook, fast.** "The theme has got to be catchy from the start ... a call-sign" (Tony Hatch); "a very strong hook,
  sonically and melodically" (Simon May). [BBC Radio 2: 10 rules](https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/articles/17HqKxj9gvLxTRnRqqn9TH3/10-rules-for-writing-a-memorable-theme-tune)
- **Short.** TV themes make their impression in ~10-15 s ([RTS, David Lowe](https://rts.org.uk/article/working-lives-tv-theme-composer-david-lowe));
  podcast openers are 15-30 s with a 5-10 s sting that never changes ([Podcast Consultant](https://thepodcastconsultant.com/blog/podcast-theme-song),
  [Pixel Samy](https://pixelsamy.com/podcast/podcast-intro-guide)). Narrative shows can run longer when the open is content.
- **Sonic branding.** A theme is a "musical logo" or "sonic image" (a recognizable sound, e.g. the Law & Order gavel)
  ([Berklee TV music primer](https://college.berklee.edu/bt/231/tv_music_primer.html)); a 2-5 s mnemonic plus a longer
  brand theme built from it ([Swell: sonic branding](https://swellmusicsound.com/learn-more/what-is-sonic-branding/)).
  It must set the tone of the show and be used identically every time ([NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/lifestyle/theme-work-the-secret-to-televisions-catchiest-tunes/HJVIAWEM6XT7DB2X5ANP6UJIYI/)).
- **Structure and endings.** In a ~30 s theme, bring in something new every couple of bars, build, and end on a real
  button: "theme music rarely works with a fade" ([Sound on Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/composing-theme-music-royal-gardeners)).
- **Leitmotif.** A short recurring idea tied to an idea or character, recognizable when re-orchestrated
  ([Wikipedia: Leitmotif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leitmotif)). A 4-note motif can return in the episode score.

## Indian devotional / epic references
- **B.R. Chopra Mahabharat (1988)**, "Atha Shree Mahabharat Katha" (Raj Kamal, sung by Mahendra Kapoor, words by Pt.
  Narendra Sharma): the one-word refrain "Mahabharat" is the hook; Gita verses (4.7-8 "Yada yada...") are sung by the
  same powerful solo voice, held long, high register, melismatic; tanpura, sitar, flute, percussion, conch, swarmandal and
  synth strings/fanfares; a pre-planned modular score reused across episodes ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahabharat_(1988_TV_series)),
  [Grokipedia music section](https://grokipedia.com/page/Mahabharat_(1988_TV_series))). Each episode then opens with
  Harish Bhimani's spoken "Main Samay hoon" over music: spoken gravity right after the sung hook.
- **Ramayan (1987)**, "Mangal Bhavan Amangal Hari" (Ravindra Jain): a short, simple, singable line in 8 beats that the
  whole country can repeat; the first words are the hook ([UW Mangalacaran](https://uw.manifoldapp.org/read/mangalacaran-auspicious-beginnning/section/eed79f56-fff5-425c-b00e-f5b59e19c25c/resource/9f9c8cf1-bbfd-41cc-85de-7d8e25d738b6),
  [sargam](https://sangeetbook.com/mangal-bhavan-amangal-hari-sargam-notes/)).
- **Baahubali** devotional pieces ("Siva Sivaya Potri", "Kaun Hai Woh"): chant + choir + heavy drums + nadaswaram,
  call-and-response between a lead and a chorus ([Behindwoods review](https://www.behindwoods.com/tamil-movies/baahubali/baahubali-songs-review.html),
  [HT: Kailash Kher](https://www.hindustantimes.com/music/singing-baahubali-s-shiv-stotram-gave-me-goosebumps-kailash-kher/story-GiLTn8Nyk1U3Zi9vXU1jXO.html)).
- **Pattern:** one short repeated word or phrase carries the identity; the shloka itself is delivered slowly by one
  voice (sung or declaimed); orchestra adds conch, drums and strings for weight; the ending lands on a cadence/bell.

## Raga mood
- **Bhairav** (S r G m P d N S): "grave in mood ... seriousness, introversion, devotional"; rasa *shaant aur gambhir*
  ([Wikipedia: Bhairav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhairav_(raga)), [Rags Around the Clock](https://books.openbookpublishers.com/10.11647/obp.0313/ch2.xhtml)).
- **Darbari Kanada**: "a grave raga ... unparalleled in gravitas", worked in the lower octave with slow andolan on komal
  ga and dha ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darbari_Kanada), [Parrikar](https://www.parrikar.org/hindustani/kanada/)).
- For a warning about the fall into ruin, a **descending Bhairav cadence onto Sa through komal Re** gives gravity and
  sorrow in four notes.

## Design brief (v6)
1. **One hook = one word:** प्र-ण-श्य-ति (*pra-ṇa-śya-ti*, "he perishes") on a 4-note falling Bhairav motif **m G r S**;
   it is the leitmotif, also played by instruments and usable in episode scores and the ending sting.
2. **Diction first:** the verse is *spoken* by the approved Krishna voice (clean takes); only the hook word (and short
   repeated words) are sung, by a doubled/choir texture where the Sanskrit survives.
3. **Short:** 30-45 s total; the motif is heard in the first 5 s and at the end; ending sting ≤ 20 s.
4. **Structure:** sting (conch + drum hit) -> 2.62 spoken over a low ostinato -> build on the 2.63 chain -> call-and-
   response (Krishna: "buddhināśāt" / choir: "praṇaśyati") -> button (drum + bell on Sa), no fade.
5. **Weight:** low register, Bhairav colour (komal re/dha), slow pulse (~60 bpm, deep drum on the downbeats), strings
   rising in steps toward the climax; real dynamics (quiet 2.62, loud 2.63).
6. **Signature sounds:** shankh at the start, temple bell on the final Sa: identical every episode.
7. **Works as a recurring intro:** fades nowhere; ends on a clean button that can crossfade into Sauti's first line.
